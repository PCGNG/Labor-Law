NORMAL HOURS OF WORK  

▪	8 hours a day  
▪	Minimizes unemployment by forcing employers to utilize different shifts of employees  

Compressed work week - DOLE encourages employers and workers to enter into voluntary agreements adopting it 
▪	To promote business competitiveness and productivity, improve efficiency by lower operating costs and reduce work-related expenses of employees 
▪	An alternative arrangement = normal working week is reduced to less than 6 days but total working hours remains at 48 hours  

Conditions of a valid CWW scheme 
1.	Expressly and voluntarily supported by majority of the employees affected 
2.	If work is hazardous, a certification is needed from an accredited safety organization or the firm's safety committee that work beyond 8 hours is within the limits or level of exposure set by DOLE's occupational safety and health standards 
3.	Should not result in diminution of benefits 
4.	The DOLE is duly notified  

HOURS OF WORK INCLUDE:  
1.	All the time which an employee is required to be on duty or to be at a prescribed workplace 
2.	All time during which an employee is suffered or permitted to work 
3.	Rest period of short duration not exceeding 20 minutes 
4.	Meal periods of less than 20 minutes are only rest periods and thus considered as hours of work  
Principles in determining hours worked: 
1.	All hours required to give to the employer whether productive labor or mental exertion 
2.	Employee need not leave his workplace for rest periods 
3.	If work benefited or is necessary to the er 
4.	No replacement 
5.	Inactive by reason of interruptions beyond his control

Waiting Time
♣	Waiting time spent by an employee shall be considered as working time if waiting is an integral part of his work or the employee is required or engaged by the employer to wait. (Section 5[a], Rule I, Ibid.)  
♣	An employee who is required to remain on call in the employer's premises or so close thereto that he cannot use the time effectively and gainfully for his own purpose shall be considered as working while on call. An employee who is not required to leave word at his home or with company officials where he may be reached is not working while on call.
♣	Employees who are "on-call" but are required to remain in or near the work premises, respond to calls within a short timeframe, or refrain from personal activities that would prevent them from responding promptly are generally considered to be working during their on-call hours. Merely being on the employer's roster of potential responders without any immediate requirement to remain in a specific place or state of readiness may not, by itself, constitute compensable waiting time. It is the level of restriction on the employee's freedom that matters.

PRELIMINARY & POSTLIMINARY ACTIVITIES (COMPENSABLE TIME)

1.	Controlled or required by employer; AND  
2.	Are pursued necessarily and primarily for the employer's benefit.

Arica v. NLRC 
(GR No. 78210, Feb. 28, 1989)
The 30-minute assembly time practiced by the employees of the company cannot be considered as "waiting time" since they are not subject to the absolute control of the company during this period, otherwise, their failure to report in the assembly time would justify the company to impose disciplinary measures.

Travel time
1.	From home to work – 
GR: not compensable
XPN:
1. Where the worker is made to work on an emergency call and travel is necessary in proceeding to the workplace; 
2. Travel is done through a conveyance provided by the employer; 
3. Travel is done under the supervision and control of the employer; and 
4. Travel is done under vexing and dangerous circumstance;
2.	Travel in all day's work – compensable (part of principal activity)
3.	Travel away from home – compensable If it cuts across an employee's workday because it substitutes for the hours that the employee should have been in the office.

Power Interruption
1.	First 20 MINUTES is COMPENSABLE;  
2.	Succeeding minutes NOT COMPENSABLE;  
3.	If despite the lapse of the 1st 20 minutes, the employees are required to stay in the workplace, such time is COMPENSABLE.    

SEMESTRAL BREAK OF TEACHERS  
	Apply to regular employees only. 
	Considered as an interruption of work beyond their control

WEBINAR
NOT COMPENSABLE IF:  
1.Attendance is OUTSIDE of the employee's regular working hours;  
2.Attendance is in fact VOLUNTARY;  
3.Employee does not perform any productive work during such  attendance.
