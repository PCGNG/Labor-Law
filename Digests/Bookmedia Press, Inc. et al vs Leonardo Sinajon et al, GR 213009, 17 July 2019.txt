Component	Content
Case Details	Bookmedia Press, Inc. and Yan Brizuela v. Leonardo Sinajon and Yanly Abenir

G.R. No. 213009, July 17, 2019

Third Division, Peralta, J.
Facts	Leonardo Sinajon and Yanly Abenir were employed as in-house security personnel by Bookmedia Press, Inc. (Bookmedia). On July 20, 1997, Bookmedia received a report from another security guard stating that Sinajon and Abenir punched in their time cards and then immediately left company premises, with Sinajon returning in the evening to punch out their time cards. When directed to explain, the guards explained that they had to leave due to family emergencies: Abenir received an urgent call that his brother was in trouble and asked someone to punch his card, while Sinajon left to fix his house roof after it was damaged right before an impending storm and to tend to his sick wife, returning later that afternoon to wait for his replacement. Bookmedia found their acts fraudulent and summarily dismissed both guards the following day for serious misconduct, willful disobedience, and dishonesty under Article 282 [now Art. 297] of the Labor Code. Both guards filed complaints for illegal dismissal. The Labor Arbiter and NLRC held the dismissal illegal because the single infraction did not warrant the penalty of termination. The CA affirmed.
Issues	1. Whether a single incident of leaving work early due to family emergencies and punching out time cards warrants the penalty of dismissal under Article 297 of the Labor Code.

2. Whether actual reinstatement remains appropriate considering the lapse of over 22 years of litigation.
Held	1. The dismissal was ILLEGAL; the penalty of dismissal is disproportionate to an isolated infraction prompted by family emergencies.

2. Reinstatement is NO LONGER VIABLE due to strained relations and the long passage of time (22 years); separation pay in lieu of reinstatement is awarded.


Verbatim Legal Basis:

Article 297 [formerly Article 282], Labor Code of the Philippines:

"Art. 297. Termination by employer. — An employer may terminate an employment for any of the following causes:

(a) Serious misconduct or willful disobedience by the employee of the lawful orders of his employer or representative in connection with his work;

(b) Gross and habitual neglect by the employee of his duties;

(c) Fraud or willful breach by the employee of the trust reposed in him by his employer or duly authorized representative;

(e) Other causes analogous to the foregoing."


Article 294 [formerly Article 279], Labor Code of the Philippines:

"Art. 294. Security of tenure. — ... An employee who is unjustly dismissed from work shall be entitled to reinstatement without loss of seniority rights and other privileges and to his full backwages, inclusive of allowances, and to his other benefits or their monetary equivalent computed from the time his compensation was withheld from him up to the time of his actual reinstatement."
Ratio Decidendi	1. Proportionality of Penalty to Infraction: While an employer has the prerogative to discipline its workforce, dismissal is the ultimate penalty and must be meted out only for grave causes. For misconduct or neglect to justify dismissal, it must be serious and habitual. An isolated incident of leaving work early due to sudden family emergencies does not show a perverse and wrongful attitude that renders the employee completely unfit to work. Bookmedia presented no evidence that the guards had repeatedly committed this infraction in the past.

2. Strained Relations and Passage of Time in Lieu of Reinstatement: Reinstatement is the primary statutory relief for an illegally dismissed employee. However, when reinstatement becomes impossible, impractical, or unfeasible due to strained relations caused by litigation, separation pay at the rate of one month salary for every year of service is awarded. With over 22 years having elapsed since dismissal, and considering that their duties as in-house guards involved securing the physical safety of company executives, forcing reinstatement is no longer feasible.
Fallo	WHEREFORE, premises considered, the petition is DENIED. The January 23, 2014 Decision and July 3, 2014 Resolution of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. SP No. 128362 are AFFIRMED with MODIFICATION in that the award of reinstatement is DELETED. In lieu thereof, petitioner Bookmedia Press, Inc. is ORDERED to pay respondents Leonardo Sinajon and Yanly Abenir separation pay computed at the rate of one (1) month salary for every year of service until the finality of this Decision, along with full backwages from the time of their illegal dismissal up to the finality of this Decision, with legal interest of six percent (6%) per annum on all monetary awards from finality until fully paid. The case is REMANDED to the Labor Arbiter for the computation of the amounts.


SO ORDERED.
