Benguet Consolidated, Inc. v. Social Security SystemG.R. No. L-19254, March 31, 1964 (10 SCRA 618)   
Facts
Benguet Consolidated, Inc. was a mining enterprise employing thousands of daily-wage and regular workers. When the Social Security Act of 1954 (R.A. No. 1161) took effect, the Social Security Commission issued a circular demanding compulsory employer-employee contribution payments for all workers in the company's employ. Benguet Consolidated challenged the validity of the law and the compulsory coverage of its workers, arguing that: (1) compulsory membership unconstitutionally impairs existing collective bargaining agreements and private pension programs already established by the company; and (2) the law constitutes an invalid exercise of the State's police power and an arbitrary deprivation of property without due process of law.

Issue
Whether the compulsory coverage of employers and private employees under the Social Security Law is a valid exercise of police power and whether it unconstitutionally impairs private employment contracts and existing company retirement plans.

Held
The Social Security Law is a VALID and CONSTITUTIONAL exercise of the police power of the State; it does NOT unconstitutionally impair private contracts or retirement funds.

Verbatim Legal Basis:

Section 9, Republic Act No. 1161 (The Social Security Act):

"Sec. 9. Compulsory Coverage. — Coverage in the SSS shall be compulsory upon all employees not over sixty years of age and their employers: Provided, That any benefit already granted by an employer to his employees more favorable than those provided under this Act shall not be discontinued or reduced by reason of the enactment hereof..."

Ratio
1. Primacy of Police Power over Private Contracts: The Social Security Act was enacted pursuant to the social justice and welfare mandates of the Constitution. Private contracts, including CBAs and voluntary company pension agreements, are necessarily subordinate to the police power of the State enacted for the general welfare and economic security of the working class.


2. Non-Diminution of Superior Private Benefits: The law expressly provides that preexisting private retirement or benefit funds that offer advantages superior to the statutory minimum are not abolished or extinguished; rather, statutory SSS coverage operates concurrently to guarantee basic social insurance against death, disability, sickness, and old age.

Fallo
WHEREFORE, the decision appealed from is hereby AFFIRMED, with costs against petitioner-appellant Benguet Consolidated, Inc.
