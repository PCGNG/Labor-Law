Union School International, Pastor Abraham Cho, Jaime Nabua, and Jennifer Mandapat v. Charley Jane Dagdag
G.R. No. 234186, November 21, 2018
First Division, Tijam, J.
Facts
Charley Jane Dagdag was a probationary elementary school teacher at Union School International. During her employment, she became pregnant out of wedlock. The father of the unborn child was an unmarried man. Upon learning of her pregnancy, the school administration convened a disciplinary grievance meeting with Dagdag. The school authorities told her that getting pregnant out of wedlock constituted "gross immorality" under school policies and the Code of Ethics for Professional Teachers. Dagdag was presented with an ultimatum: tender her voluntary resignation or be subjected to formal administrative dismissal proceedings. Faced with this choice, Dagdag filed a complaint for constructive illegal dismissal. The school contended that pregnancy out of wedlock is a ground to terminate a probationary teacher for failure to meet institutional moral standards.
Issue
Whether an unmarried teacher who becomes pregnant out of wedlock by an unmarried partner can be forced to resign or dismissed on the ground of gross immorality under the Labor Code.
Held
The teacher was CONSTRUCTIVELY and ILLEGALLY DISMISSED; pregnancy out of wedlock without legal impediment to marry is NOT gross immorality, and forcing her to choose between resignation and dismissal violates Article 135 [now 133] of the Labor Code.


Verbatim Legal Basis:

Article 135 [now Article 133], Labor Code of the Philippines:

"Art. 135. Discrimination prohibited. — It shall be unlawful for any employer to discriminate against any woman employee with respect to terms and conditions of employment solely on account of her sex."


Article 137 [now Article 135] (2), Labor Code of the Philippines:

"Art. 137. Prohibited acts. — It shall be unlawful for any employer: ... (2) To discharge such woman on account of her pregnancy, or while on leave or in confinement due to her pregnancy..."
Ratio Decidendi
1. Secular Standard of Morality (Leus and Capin-Cadiz Doctrines Applied): To constitute a valid ground for dismissal, "grossly immoral conduct" must be judged by a public, secular standard, not a religious one. It must be so corrupt or depraved as to be reprehensible to a high degree. Where both parents are single adults without legal impediment to marry, engaging in consensual sexual relations resulting in pregnancy out of wedlock does not constitute gross immorality under the Labor Code.

2. Involuntary Resignation as Constructive Dismissal: Constructive dismissal occurs when an employee is placed in a situation where resignation is the only available option. Forcing a teacher to resign under the pretext of avoiding an administrative dismissal record due to pregnancy constitutes prohibited gender discrimination and unlawful constructive discharge.
Fallo
WHEREFORE, the Petition is DENIED. The Decision dated November 10, 2016 and the Resolution dated August 17, 2017 of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. SP No. 133482 are AFFIRMED with MODIFICATION. Petitioner Union School International is ordered to pay respondent Charley Jane Dagdag full backwages, separation pay in lieu of reinstatement, moral damages, exemplary damages, and attorney's fees, with legal interest of six percent (6%) per annum from finality of this Decision until fully paid.


SO ORDERED.
