Government Service Insurance System v. Aurelia Y. Calumpiano G.R. No. 196102, November 26, 2014
Facts
Aurelia Y. Calumpiano served as a Court Stenographer in the Regional Trial Court for thirty (30) years (from 1972 to 2002). Her work required taking stenographic notes during daily court sessions, sitting in prolonged static postures, enduring high stress from transcription deadlines, and spending long hours deciphering stenographic tapes and typing transcripts. During her service, she developed essential hypertension and began losing her eyesight. She was clinically diagnosed with open-angle glaucoma, hypertensive retinopathy, and posterior vitreous detachment, leaving her blind. She retired on disability and filed a claim for permanent total disability benefits under P.D. No. 626 with the GSIS. GSIS and ECC denied the claim, stating that open-angle glaucoma is not an occupational disease and has no causal relation to stenographic work. The CA reversed, ruling that essential hypertension is listed as compensable and that her advanced retinal and ocular damage flowed from hypertensive vascular strain.
Issue
Whether the open-angle glaucoma and vision impairment of a court stenographer suffering from essential hypertension is compensable under P.D. No. 626.
Held
The condition is COMPENSABLE; the severe visual impairment flowed from essential hypertension, which is a listed occupational disease under Annex "A" of the Rules.
Verbatim Legal Basis:
Item 29 (Essential Hypertension), Annex "A", Amended Rules on Employees' Compensation:
"29. Essential Hypertension. — Hypertension will be considered as an occupational disease if: (a) It occurs in employees whose job requires sustained mental and physical stress, such as air traffic controllers, court stenographers, and similar occupations; (b) Blood pressure measurements confirm high levels during employment; and (c) Resulting end-organ damage (heart, eyes, brain, kidneys) is substantiated."
Ratio
1. Consequences Flowing from Listed Occupational Diseases: Under the Employees' Compensation Program, all medical complications and end-organ consequences that naturally flow from a primary compensable injury or illness are compensable. Essential hypertension is an enumerated occupational disease for stressful court duties.
2. Reasonable Work-Connection: A court stenographer is subjected to sustained mental and physical strain meeting judicial deadlines. When long-standing work-induced hypertension damages the delicate ocular vasculature leading to hypertensive retinopathy and accelerated glaucomatous blindness, the requirement of reasonable work connection is met by substantial evidence.
Fallo
WHEREFORE, the Petition is DENIED. The October 22, 2010 Decision and March 8, 2011 Resolution of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. SP No. 106093 are AFFIRMED. The Government Service Insurance System is ORDERED to PAY respondent Aurelia Y. Calumpiano the disability compensation benefits due her under Presidential Decree No. 626, as amended.
