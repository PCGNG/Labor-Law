Julius B. Campol v. Mayor Ronald S. Balao-as and Vice-Mayor Dominador I. Sianen

G.R. No. 197634, November 28, 2016

Third Division, Jardeleza, J.
Facts	Julius B. Campol was the permanent Secretary to the Sangguniang Bayan (Salary Grade 24) of the Municipality of Boliney, Abra. In 2004, following local elections, newly assumed municipal authorities dropped Campol from the civil service rolls on the ground of continuous absence without approved leave (AWOL) for two months. Campol appealed his separation, arguing that he was on approved sick leave supported by medical certificates and that his dropping from the rolls was politically motivated and executed without due process. The Civil Service Commission (CSC) affirmed his termination, but the Court of Appeals reversed the CSC, ruling that Campol was illegally dropped from the rolls and unjustly dismissed. However, the CA refused to order his reinstatement because Campol had obtained interim employment as an administrative officer (Salary Grade 4) with the Public Attorney's Office (PAO) in October 2005 out of economic survival after his wife was murdered, and limited his backwages to the period from dismissal until October 2005 (when he joined PAO). Campol appealed to the Supreme Court seeking full reinstatement and uninterrupted backwages until actual reinstatement.
Issues	1. Whether an illegally dismissed public/civil service employee who accepts interim employment in another government office forfeits his constitutional right to reinstatement.

2. Whether the backwages of an illegally dismissed public servant are subject to deductions for earnings obtained from other employment, or subject to a five-year cap.
Held	1. The public servant is ENTITLED TO REINSTATEMENT; accepting lower-salaried interim employment out of economic survival does NOT forfeit security of tenure or waive the right to reinstatement.

2. The public servant is entitled to FULL BACKWAGES from illegal dismissal until actual reinstatement, WITHOUT DEDUCTIONS of interim earnings and WITHOUT any five-year ceiling.


Verbatim Legal Basis:

Article IX-B, Section 2(3), 1987 Philippine Constitution:

"No officer or employee of the civil service shall be removed or suspended except for cause provided by law."


Section 47, Book V, Title I, Subtitle A, Executive Order No. 292 (Administrative Code of 1987):

"Sec. 47. Disciplinary Jurisdiction. — ... An employee who is exonerated on appeal shall be restored to his former position and paid back salaries and other emoluments corresponding to the period he was illegally separated from the service."
Ratio Decidendi	1. Right to Security of Tenure in Public Sector: An employee of the civil service who is invalidly dismissed or dropped from the rolls without legal cause is entitled to be restored to his office. The fact that he secured interim employment to feed his family does not constitute a waiver of tenurial rights. To retain the reinstated post, he is simply required to resign from his interim employment upon actual reinstatement.

2. Repudiation of the Old 5-Year Cap and Deductions in Public Sector Back Salaries: The Supreme Court reexamined historical precedents (Cristobal v. Melchor and Tan v. Gimenez) and ruled that an illegally dismissed civil servant who is ordered reinstated is entitled to the full payment of backwages for the entire duration he was wrongfully kept away from public service, without deduction of income earned elsewhere and without the obsolete five-year cap. Just as in the private sector under the Bustamante doctrine, an employee should not be penalized for seeking interim work during protracted litigation.
Fallo	WHEREFORE, the Petition is GRANTED. The Court of Appeals' Decision dated December 15, 2010 in CA-G.R. SP No. 106097 is REVERSED insofar as it did not order Campol's reinstatement and limited the award of backwages to cover only the period from his dismissal until his new employment. This Court ORDERS Campol's reinstatement to the position of Sangguniang Bayan Secretary of the Municipality of Boliney, Abra, provided that he first resigns from his current employment. This Court also AWARDS Campol backwages to be computed from the time that he was illegally dropped from the rolls until he is reinstated to his position, with legal interest of six percent (6%) per annum from finality until paid.


SO ORDERED.
