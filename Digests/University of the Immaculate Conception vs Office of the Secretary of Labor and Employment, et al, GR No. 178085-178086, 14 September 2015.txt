University of the Immaculate Conception v. Office of the Secretary of Labor and Employment, et al.

G.R. Nos. 178085-178086, September 14, 2015

Third Division, Jardeleza, J.
Facts	In 1994, during collective bargaining negotiations between the University of the Immaculate Conception (UIC) and the union, the union staged a strike over economic issues and the exclusion of certain supervisory and confidential employees from the bargaining unit. The Secretary of Labor assumed jurisdiction under Article 263(g) [now Art. 278(g)] of the Labor Code and issued a Return-to-Work Order. Meanwhile, an independent Voluntary Arbitration panel sustained UIC's position that twelve (12) positions were confidential and excluded from the union. When the twelve employees insisted on retaining union membership, UIC dismissed them. The Secretary of Labor subsequently issued an order directing UIC to reinstate the twelve dismissed employees with backwages pending the resolution of their termination dispute. In 2004, the Secretary also constituted a Tripartite Committee to compute the tuition fee incremental proceeds due to employees under R.A. No. 6728, resulting in an award of P11,070,473.00 against UIC. UIC appealed via Rule 65, arguing that: (a) the Secretary of Labor had no jurisdiction to order the reinstatement of the twelve employees because their termination was an incidental dispute governed by the prior Voluntary Arbitration award; and (b) the Secretary lacked authority under Article 263(g) to create a Tripartite Committee to compute tuition increments. The CA affirmed the Secretary’s orders.
Issues	1. Does the Secretary of Labor’s assumption of jurisdiction under Article 263(g) [now Art. 278(g)] extend to incidental issues, such as the termination of individual employees occurring after the assumption order?

2. Does the Secretary of Labor have the authority under Article 263(g) to constitute a Tripartite Committee to audit and compute statutory tuition fee incremental proceeds?
Held	1. The Secretary of Labor HAS INCIDENTAL JURISDICTION; the assumption power under Article 263(g) extends to all issues incidental to or arising from the labor dispute, including subsequent employee dismissals.

2. The Secretary HAS THE AUTHORITY to constitute a Tripartite Committee to determine net incremental proceeds as part of resolving the overall economic conflict.


Verbatim Legal Basis:

Article 278 [formerly Article 263] (g), Labor Code of the Philippines:

"Art. 278. Strikes, picketing and lockouts. — ... (g) When, in his opinion, there exists a labor dispute causing or likely to cause a strike or lockout in an industry indispensable to the national interest, the Secretary of Labor and Employment may assume jurisdiction over the dispute and decide the same or certify the same to the Commission for compulsory arbitration... Such assumption or certification shall have the effect of automatically enjoining the intended or impending strike or lockout as specified in the assumption or certification order."
Ratio Decidendi	1. Doctrine of Incidental Jurisdiction: The assumption of jurisdiction by the Secretary of Labor under Article 263(g) is broad and comprehensive. It encompasses not merely the core strike issues stated in the initial notice of strike, but all collateral, subsequent, and incidental issues arising between the parties during the dispute. The termination of the twelve employees was an outgrowth of the representation controversy involved in the strike; hence, the Secretary possessed incidental jurisdiction to review the dismissal and order payroll/actual reinstatement to maintain industrial peace.

2. Power to Form Fact-Finding Committees: Resolving CBA economic disputes in educational institutions frequently requires verifying tuition fee increases and student fee shares under R.A. No. 6728. The Secretary possesses the power to devise mechanisms, including the creation of a Tripartite Committee, to ascertain financial facts and audit books to achieve a fair resolution of the labor dispute.
Fallo	WHEREFORE, the Petition for Review on Certiorari is DENIED. The Decisions dated April 24, 2007 and Resolutions dated May 31, 2007 of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. SP Nos. 01396-MIN and 01398-MIN are hereby AFFIRMED.


SO ORDERED.
