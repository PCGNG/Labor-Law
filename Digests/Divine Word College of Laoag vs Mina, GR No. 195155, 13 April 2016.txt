Divine Word College of Laoag v. Shirley B. Mina

G.R. No. 195155, April 13, 2016

First Division, Reyes, J.
Facts	Shirley B. Mina was a high school teacher at the Divine Word College of Laoag (DWCL). In 2003, after teaching for 29 years, she applied for optional early retirement under the school's retirement plan, which permitted retirement after 25 years of continuous service. The school administration accepted her application, relieved her of teaching duties, and computed her retirement benefits. Later, the College refused to disburse her retirement package, claiming that during an audit, it discovered that she had participated in unauthorized financial transactions and failed to account for class collections, which the school treated as serious misconduct. DWCL served her a notice of dismissal and forfeited her retirement pay. Mina filed an illegal dismissal and retirement pay complaint. The Labor Arbiter, NLRC, and Court of Appeals ruled that the post-retirement dismissal was invalid and ordered the payment of full retirement benefits.
Issue	Whether an educational institution can dismiss an employee and forfeit her retirement benefits based on charges initiated after her optional retirement had already been approved.
Held	The forfeiture of retirement benefits was INVALID; once an employee's application for optional early retirement is approved, the employment relationship is severed by retirement, and the employer cannot subsequently dismiss the employee to forfeit accrued benefits.


Verbatim Legal Basis:

Article 302 [formerly Article 287], Labor Code of the Philippines (as amended by R.A. No. 7641):

"Art. 302. Retirement. — Any employee may be retired upon reaching the retirement age established in the collective bargaining agreement or other applicable employment contract... An employee upon reaching the age of sixty (60) years or more, but not beyond sixty-five (65) years... who has served at least five (5) years... may retire and shall be entitled to retirement pay..."
Ratio Decidendi	1. Cessation of Employer-Employee Relationship Upon Retirement: When an application for early retirement under a valid plan is approved by the employer, the employment relationship ends by retirement. The retiree ceases to be subject to the employer's disciplinary authority.

2. Vested Property Right: Retirement pay is property earned through years of continuous labor. An employer cannot resurrect past, unproven operational infractions after retirement approval as a pretext to confiscate vested retirement pay. Any remaining financial claims of the employer must be recovered through independent civil actions, not through the forfeiture of retirement benefits.
Fallo	WHEREFORE, the Petition for Review on Certiorari is DENIED. The Decision dated June 30, 2010 and Resolution dated January 12, 2011 of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. SP No. 109852 are AFFIRMED.


SO ORDERED.
