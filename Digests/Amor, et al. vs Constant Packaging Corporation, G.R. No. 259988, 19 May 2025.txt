Rhoda P. Amor, Lourdes R. Balanquit, Gloria G. Narag, Jovelyn G. Bueno, Agnes B. Ricerra, Valentina H. Adalid, Elynor R. Romero, Maria Fe J. Claro, Elizabeth C. Catulay, Celestina V. Magtolis, Jonalyn R. Romero, Jasmin B. Borlagdatan-Arambulo, Marissa C. Busel, and Glenda M. Tordillo v. Constant Packaging Corporation, William Chan, and Virginia Chan
G.R. No. 259988, May 19, 2025
Second Division, Leonen, SAJ.

Facts
Petitioners were hired by Constant Packaging Corporation (Constant Packaging), a printer of packaging materials, as sorters, revisers, and packers paid on a pakyaw (piece-rate) basis. Petitioners submitted a petition to management raising grievances regarding wages below minimum, 12-hour shifts, 7-day workweeks, delayed salaries, and non-remittance of SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG contributions. Management instructed them to leave if dissatisfied. Tensions escalated when several petitioners were barred from entering company premises after missing the company Christmas party. When other petitioners filed formal complaints with the Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE) and summonses were served, company security guards prohibited twelve of the workers from entering the plant to work. Two workers (Busel and Tordillo) resigned due to health issues and supervisor pressure. Constant Packaging contended that there was no dismissal because the pakyaw workers were free to come and go, work was available, and their positions were never terminated. The Labor Arbiter found illegal dismissal, but the NLRC and Court of Appeals reversed, holding that security guards barring entry did not constitute an overt act of dismissal and that petitioners had abandoned their work.
Issue
Whether preventing workers from entering the company premises when they are ready and willing to work constitutes actual illegal dismissal, or whether the workers are guilty of abandonment.
Held
The act of barring employees from entering the workplace constitutes ACTUAL ILLEGAL DISMISSAL; the employer failed to establish abandonment or compliance with substantive and procedural due process.
Verbatim Legal Basis:

Article 294 [formerly Article 279], Labor Code of the Philippines:

"Art. 294. Security of tenure. — In cases of regular employment, the employer shall not terminate the services of an employee except for a just cause or when authorized by this Title. An employee who is unjustly dismissed from work shall be entitled to reinstatement without loss of seniority rights and other privileges and to his full backwages, inclusive of allowances, and to his other benefits or their monetary equivalent computed from the time his compensation was withheld from him up to the time of his actual reinstatement."


Article 297 [formerly Article 282] (b), Labor Code of the Philippines:

"Art. 297. Termination by employer. — An employer may terminate an employment for any of the following causes: ... (b) Gross and habitual neglect by the employee of his duties..."
Ratio Decidendi
1. Barring Entry as an Overt Act of Dismissal: The best evidence of dismissal is ordinarily a formal written notice; however, its absence does not mean dismissal did not occur. When workers who are able and willing to work report to the company gate and are arbitrarily turned away and physically prevented from entering by company security guards acting on management instructions, the physical barring constitutes an unambiguous overt act of dismissal.

2. Failure of the Defense of Abandonment: To sustain a defense of abandonment, the employer must prove: (a) failure to report for work or absence without valid or justifiable reason; and (b) clear and deliberate intent to sever the employer-employee relationship through an overt act. Proceeding to the company premises to work and promptly filing complaints with the DOLE and the NLRC disprove any intention to abandon employment. Because the dismissal was devoid of both just cause and the mandatory two-notice rule, it was illegal.
Fallo
WHEREFORE, the Petition for Review on Certiorari is PARTIALLY GRANTED. The Decision dated September 27, 2021 and Resolution dated March 30, 2022 of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. SP No. 164287 are REVERSED and SET ASIDE.
Petitioners Rhoda P. Amor, Lourdes R. Balanquit, Gloria G. Narag, Jovelyn G. Bueno, Agnes B. Ricerra, Valentina H. Adalid, Elynor R. Romero, Maria Fe J. Claro, Elizabeth C. Catulay, Celestina V. Magtolis, Jonalyn R. Romero, and Jasmin B. Borlagdatan-Arambulo are found to have been ILLEGALLY DISMISSED. Respondent Constant Packaging Corporation is ORDERED TO PAY petitioners their separation pay in lieu of reinstatement, full backwages from the time they were illegally dismissed until the finality of this Decision, and attorney's fees equivalent to ten percent (10%) of the total monetary award.
The monetary awards shall earn legal interest of six percent (6%) per annum from the date of finality of this Decision until full satisfaction.
SO ORDERED.
